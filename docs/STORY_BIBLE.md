# Space & Crafts — Story Spine

## The promise

Splat is a teenage alien inventor and patchmaker who maps the rainbow roads with Vesper, their best friend and shortcut rival. Splat’s brother Squat is part of the family behind the adventure. Splat and Vesper travel with a small maker caravan, fixing gateways wherever the road frays. Before a scouting trip, they agree on a simple fallback signal: three taps on the gateway beacon say “I’m here. Keep looking.”

Their first route leads into the Rainbow Kingdom. There, a spreading fault called the Unravel pulls loose pieces of connected worlds into storms. The old gatekeepers respond by sealing damaged arches. Their caution keeps each local world safe for a while, but strands the people on either side and leaves the network weaker. Splat sets out to answer Vesper’s signal and learn why the roads are failing.

## World 2 — Beeping Caves Kingdom

The Beeping Caves Kingdom is the second realm, centered on the Chime Hollows: a chain of deep blue caverns where small resonant crystals keep the safe routes marked with a familiar two-note “bip-bip.” A fault in the Unravel has knocked the signals out of tune. The cave paths still connect, but their anchor rings are hard to read until Splat learns their pulse pattern. Each important beep has a matching visible ring and bright anchor response; the player never needs hearing alone to find a route.

Splat gathers a winding spool, a rounded tuning-fork hook, and a ping crystal to build the **Pingline Spooler**. It sends a short audible-and-visible ping toward a resonant anchor, then reels Splat toward a broad, safe ledge. The invention supports exploration and traversal and is also used in the World 2 boss exam. The proposed gatekeeper is the existing folded-paper **Perforation Warden** candidate: its stamped folds mute the chime routes as a misguided attempt to keep the cave from collapsing. This is still a design assignment, not a scene/AI/encounter integration; the Warden's current authored action is not strong enough to treat as production-ready.

World 1 remains Rainbow Kingdom. **Leaf Lands stay later**, first represented by the World 3 Magnet Marsh; Mountain Lands remain a later destination at World 6. Pulpal and Sproutlet stay associated with Leaf Lands rather than being relabeled as cave residents.

The story’s central conflict is **connection versus protection**. Splat takes risks to reconnect worlds; the gatekeepers seal them because they fear a larger break. Splat’s inventions let both sides find a third answer: repair the route with whatever the world can spare.

- **Vesper** — Splat’s best friend and mapmaking rival, stranded beyond the first broken road. They compete over routes and shortcuts, but the three-tap signal is their shared promise to keep searching for each other. The opening and answer cards pair Splat's UI portrait with Vesper's teal mapmaker portrait; at the finale, a brief in-world portrait hologram appears above the Gateway Terrace beacon during the answering chime and expanding rings, while an on-screen message gives Vesper's written reply. - **Rattle** — Arrival’s old gateway keeper. Rattle is not hunting Splat: it believes sealing the cracking road will protect the worlds beyond and has pulled the relay’s loose parts into its scrapstorm. The opening cards now state that motive, and the field objective frames the Boing-O-Matic fight as proof the road can be mended. Encounter toasts say Rattle braces the gate, expose its relay as the target, and describe the clamp weakening with each hit. The player’s crafted launcher turns the fight into a repair test.
- **The Unravel** — The growing fault in the rainbow-road network. It is a world-scale mystery and pressure, not a speaking villain introduced before the game can show its cause.

## Arrival story beats

1. **Promise:** the first opening card calls Splat and Vesper best friends and rival mapmakers who argue over shortcuts, then states their shared three-tap promise (“I’m here. Keep looking.”), shows them together with Vesper's route map, and explains their separation; on arrival, the distant terrace beacon gives three faint visual pulses while the camera first guides toward the gate. The final card returns to both portraits as Splat answers. Portrait and pulse layout still need an in-game review.
2. **Problem:** Rattle’s storm blocks the relay. The opening now explains that Rattle seals the road because it fears the cracks will spread, while the field note frames Vesper as calling from beyond the gate and gives the player a direct goal: gather parts and build the launcher.
3. **Experiment:** the spring, dish, and charged crystal become the Boing-O-Matic. Each part is useful evidence of how the local relay works.
4. **Test:** the player rides the invention through Rattle’s attack and strikes the exposed relay. Rattle braces the gate more fiercely as the fight progresses; this uses the new tool to resolve the opening problem rather than adding a separate combat objective.
5. **Answer:** Rattle releases the gate and admits, “I was afraid the road would break. You found another way.” The dormant gateways wake while the line finishes; then the beacon answers with expanding cyan rings, a spatial rising three-note chime, and three cyan flashes. A brief toast shows Vesper's written reply: “I’m here, Splat. Keep looking.” After the three taps, Splat answers, “Signal received, map thief. I’m coming.” Splat listens toward the beacon for Vesper's response, then celebrates. Arrival's 7.1-second return delay leaves time for the response and celebration; playthrough must confirm sound level, visibility, text timing, and character poses.

## Longer arc

Each world introduces a community and a gatekeeper whose local solution to the Unravel has become a new obstacle. Splat learns that a single powerful fix cannot stabilize every road. The player combines ideas from earlier inventions, helps the gatekeepers trust one another, and restores a shared route map. The final goal is to reunite with Vesper and help the worlds choose when to open, close, or reroute a gateway together.

Keep this arc visible through short actions and character reactions. Each world should give its craft loop a story purpose, let its boss express the local conflict, and end with a meaningful change to the road map. Do not make every gatekeeper a villain or every solution another larger weapon.
